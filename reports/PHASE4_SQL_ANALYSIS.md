# Phase 4 — SQL database and supply-chain analysis

## Build, schema, and integrity

Run `python src/run_phase4.py` from the repository root to rebuild `database/novamart_scm.db` and five report CSVs from the approved Phase 3 files. `database/schema.sql` defines seven typed tables, primary and composite foreign keys, non-null/check constraints, and date/key indexes. The loader enables `PRAGMA foreign_keys=ON` for its connection before loading. It executes every statement in the six numbered SQL files and writes `reports/phase4_validation.json`. Run `python src/validate_phase4.py` for independent checks and a reproducible second build.

## Business findings from the public synthetic source

- The file records **6,750,876 units sold** across its 76,000 SKU-days. Groceries contribute **3,127,335 units**, the largest category volume. A manager would monitor grocery availability closely, while remembering the category has more local SKUs/rows than other categories; total volume is not the same as per-SKU performance.
- Location `S002` records **1,386,126 units sold**, the highest of five locations. That is a descriptive comparison of the published simulation, not an assessment of actual NOVAMART stores.
- There are **406 zero-inventory SKU-days across 69 local SKUs**. At the final source date, the 100 latest inventory snapshots total **29,049 units**. These are historical stock indicators; zero inventory at one snapshot does not prove a lost sale.
- Using latest stock divided by the prior 28 days' average units sold, **90 of 100 local SKUs** fall below an illustrative seven-day coverage threshold. This high share calls for review of the source's snapshot semantics before interpreting it as an operational shortage. It is not a forecast, safety stock calculation, or purchase-order recommendation.
- Promotion-flagged rows average **103.10 units sold** versus **81.83** without the flag. This is an association in synthetic data; product mix, timing, and the publisher's generation rules may explain the difference. SQL cannot establish promotion causality.

Monthly trends and seasonality breakdowns are in `02_demand_analysis.sql`. January 2024 ends on the 30th and should not be casually compared as a full month. `Demand` is retained only as a publisher-estimated contextual field; the 21,000 rows where sales exceed it rule out presenting it as verified orders.

## Findings within the NOVAMART simulated procurement scenario

- **5,299 simulated POs** represent **348,263,386.82 units of unspecified currency in ordered commitment** (`ordered_quantity × procurement_unit_cost`). This is not paid or received spend, and no real savings can be inferred. Procurement volumes are generated by a simple prior-sales policy and do not reconcile with published stock movements.
- **4,923 simulated orders** were fully received and **376** partially received. This is a count of complete versus partial orders; quantity-weighted fill is separately defined in the KPI dictionary.
- All **5,299 receipts** have a paired PO. **3,316** arrive on or before the expected date and **1,983** arrive later. Supplier on-time percentages range from **57.67%** (`SUP-05`) to **68.81%** (`SUP-01`) in this scenario. These differences follow assumed lead-time variation, not real supplier behavior or an external benchmark.
- In the simulated supplier table, `SUP-05` has **11.03 mean actual lead days**, **2.52-day population SD**, and **42.33% late receipts**. These calculated outcomes suggest where a planner would investigate lead-time variability if such transactions were real; they do not justify a real supplier rating.

The supplier SQL computes on-time/late rates over eligible joined receipts, actual lead-time population SD, positive delay over all receipts and over late receipts, and unit-weighted receipt fill. `reports/supplier_performance.csv` contains all six suppliers. Procurement spend by supplier, location, and category, monthly activity, and status counts are queryable in `04_procurement_analysis.sql`.

## SQL portfolio skills and exported results

The numbered files demonstrate joins with key-preserving relationships, CTEs for current versus recent history, `CASE` conditional aggregation, date arithmetic, `ROW_NUMBER()` for latest snapshot per SKU, grouped aggregates, `NULLIF` for safe coverage division, and a subquery for a shared latest date. Each statement corresponds to a business question; the seven-day filter is explicitly descriptive.
